# Mite

Open-source C# toolkit for mesh curvature analysis, form finding, gridshell net design and lath fabrication. Pure .NET with zero dependencies — runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.

## Try it in the browser

[kempffsele.me/mite](https://kempffsele.me/mite) runs Mite.Core compiled to WebAssembly: analytic shapes, a free-form loft or your own OBJ/STL, every curvature mode, all the nets including symmetric asymptotic webs and AAG webs, one lath section for every curve with cutting patterns, a beam-frame check with chosen supports and joints, and the net moving as a scissor mechanism — the same code as the plugin, nothing uploaded. Source in `src/Mite.Web`.

## Features

### Curvature Analysis
- **Principal Curvature** — k1, k2 values and directions per vertex (Rusinkiewicz 2004), with tangent-frame tensor smoothing and right-handed (D1, D2, N) frames for consistent direction fields
- **Gaussian Curvature** — angle deficit method with mixed Voronoi areas
- **Mean Curvature** — cotangent Laplacian over the same mixed Voronoi areas (H and K are consistently normalized); degenerate triangles are ignored
- **Umbilics** — flags vertices where k1 ≈ k2 (direction fields are undefined there; key for clean net layouts) and locates the isolated umbilics with their index (+½ lemon / monstar, −½ star) as the degenerate points of the principal direction field
- **Test Shape** — one of the analytic test surfaces (sphere, torus, ellipsoid, hyperboloid, catenoid, Enneper, monkey saddle, cone, vault, annulus, wave, Schwarz D, gyroid …) as a mesh with the exact K, H, k1, k2, principal and asymptotic directions at every vertex, to check the curvature components against the truth
- **Mesh Isocurves** — level sets of any per-vertex field (marching triangles); K = 0 outlines the anticlastic regions asymptotic laths can occupy

### Streamlines
- **Curvature Streamlines** — RK2 integration along principal curvature directions, from seeds or evenly spaced (AutoSpace)

### Form-Finding
- **Planarize Mesh** — planar quad-dominant meshes by guided projection (Tang et al. 2014: auxiliary face normals, Gauss–Newton on n·(vᵢ − vⱼ) = 0) with Liu et al. 2006's decaying fairness and closeness and Zadravec et al. 2010's shape-keeping fairness: planar to 1e-9 in 10–15 iterations, nodes kept on a reference surface; the old projection loop stays as Method 0
- **Minimal Surface** — exact cotangent Laplace solves with frozen weights (Pinkall–Polthier style); converges in a few iterations instead of thousands of flow steps
- **Force Density Method** — equilibrium solving for cable nets (q > 0) and compression shells (q < 0), with singular-system detection and a deterministic edge order
- **Dynamic Relaxation** — form finding by Barnes' dynamic relaxation with kinetic damping: gravity / point loads, soap-film tension, pre-tension, smoothing, and elastic laths (bending stiffness, cut lengths) for elastic gridshells
- All linear systems run on a built-in sparse envelope LDLᵀ solver (reverse Cuthill–McKee ordering): tens of thousands of unknowns solve in seconds

### Gridshells
- **Asymptotic Web** — Schling's asymptotic web: nodes every spacing along the two asymptotic curves through a seed, every other node the crossing of the curves through them — a quad net with no stubs and no T-junctions that keeps the surface's symmetry (flat points become singular nodes of valence 2n, one sector is traced and rotated; surfaces of revolution get the rotational web whose meridian diagonals are geodesics). Optional **AAG** optimisation (Schling, Wang, Hoyer & Pottmann 2022): one family of node diagonals made geodesic and every node star kept planar, for gridshells of three families of straight flat slats; **Refine** splits every web edge into 2 or 4 and re-optimises the fine net so the laths run smoothly through the joints, still asymptotic (upright) and geodesic (flat)
- **AGG Web** — asymptotic–geodesic–geodesic web (Schling, Wang, Hoyer & Pottmann 2022): takes any quad net of two lath families (Geodesic Net, Asymptotic Web, your own curves) and moves its nodes so that both families are geodesic — equal opposite angles at every node — and one family of node diagonals is asymptotic, staying close to the surface: two families of flat slats lying on the surface and one of upright slats. Exact on a developable surface with the rulings as diagonals (the cylinder web of the paper), with the errors before and after in the report
- **Asymptotic Net** — both families of asymptotic curves (zero normal curvature) for asymptotic gridshells, with combed family labels, a minimum crossing angle and evenly-spaced auto-seeding
- **Geodesic Net** — straightest geodesics for geodesic (lath) gridshells; families grow with Jacobi-field start angles for even strips, from a seed or from a border edge
- **Chebyshev Net** — equal-edge-length nets by the compass method: the kinematics of elastic gridshells bent from flat lattices; shear angle per node and Hazzidakis' ∬K dA per cell (the curvature read from the net; 2π is the budget of a single net)
- **Conjugate Net** — both principal families evenly spaced: an approximate conjugate net, the layout for planar-quad (PQ) panelization
- **Geodesic Path** — shortest geodesic between two points: the exact geodesic of the mesh by edge flips (FlipOut, Sharp & Crane 2020) from a Steiner-graph start path; the older curve shortening stays as Method 0
- **Mite Net** — one wire for a whole net: surface, family A, family B and a profile per family (A upright / B flat for the asymptotic–geodesic hybrid). Every net component has a `Net` output and the analysis, fabrication and kinetics components take it as their last input; Mite Net builds a net from curves, adds a profile, joins two nets, and classifies every lath end as on the border, on another lath (T-junction) or free. The viewport preview draws family A teal, family B magenta, T-junction ends orange and free ends red, as the web app does
- **Net Trim** — cookie-cuts a net to a boundary: trace on a larger mesh, draw the outline on or above it (closed loops, holes, an open cut border to border), keep what lies inside; every curve cut exactly at the boundary, the cut ends listed
- Tracing is scale-aware: step, step count and spacing default to values derived from the mesh, so millimetre and metre models behave the same
- Curves are continuous: with **Continuous** on (default) every traced curve runs border to border or closes on itself, and a curve that merges into a neighbour ends exactly on it as a T-junction — no laths floating in the middle of the surface. Curves meet the mesh border along their own direction (no hook, no crawl along the edge), also on the staircase borders of trimmed or subdivided quad meshes

### Analysis
- **Lath Analysis** — buildability check for strip laths: Darboux-frame decomposition (geodesic curvature, normal curvature, geodesic torsion) converted to bending strains against a material limit
- **Gridshell Analysis** — linear statics of the whole lath network as a coupled 3D beam frame: every crossing a node (inserted exactly into the laths), rigid, scissor-hinged or semi-rigid joints about the surface normal, laths stacked in layers joined through the lever arm of the stack (rigid end offsets), fixed or pinned supports, self-weight, area, line and point loads; displacements, reactions and reaction moments with force and moment equilibrium checks, and per-lath stress utilization with torsion (validated against Euler-Bernoulli closed forms)
- **Net Distributions** — the whole spread of a net, not only its extremes: spacing across each family (cut perpendicular to every lath, both sides, as the strip width of Pottmann et al. 2010), crossing angles between the families and lath utilization, as histograms (Freedman–Diaconis bins) with P5 / median / P95 and the share within ±5 % / ±7.5 % of the target spacing

### Fabrication
- **Lath Sweep** — extrudes on-surface curves (geodesic, asymptotic, streamline) into solid laths with a rectangular profile riding in the surface frame; flat mode for geodesic gridshells, upright (egg-crate) mode for asymptotic gridshells, with surface offset
- **Net Joints** — finds crossings between the lath families and builds lap-joint notch solids (half-lap for flat laths, slots for upright ones) with lap fraction and clearance, ready for boolean subtraction; with a flat family crossing an upright one, threaded joints (the slat passes through a slot in the lamella)
- **Lath Unroll** — flat 2D cutting patterns from laths (exact per-triangle isometry), laid out in a row for CNC
- **Lath Strip** — the strip a thin straight plank forms along each lath: the developable that stands upright on the surface (asymptotic laths) or lies on it (geodesic laths), with its rulings, an exact flat pattern (a straight band on asymptotic / geodesic laths), and the stretches where no developable exists (twist exceeds bend) with the edge strain the plank needs there
- **Lath Segment** — splits laths to stock length, cuts kept away from joints, with half-lap splice notch solids
- **Lath Labels** — lath IDs, label anchor points, and a CSV bill of materials
- **Lath Preview** — color-codes laths by utilization (green → red)
- **Flat Kit** — cut, assemble flat, deploy: presses an asymptotic net flat as a scissor mechanism and turns the flat state into a kit — the flat assembly drawing at 1 : 1 (joints numbered, laths labelled), every lath one straight strip with its joints at their spacing (halving slots that turn about the normal, pin holes, or a hub where the rays of a flat point meet), strips nested on laser sheets, the deployment sequence and a cut list; SVG (red cut, blue engrave), DXF R12 and CSV in millimetres
- **Net Topology** — nodes and members of a two-family net as a structural graph (for Karamba-style analysis or a node schedule); same-family crossings and T-junctions are nodes too; the panels between the laths as a quad-dominant mesh with their planarity (planar-quad check: curvature lines nearly planar, asymptotic nets skew — wire it into Planarize Mesh)

### Kinetics
- **Net Kinetics** — moves a scissor-jointed asymptotic net as a mechanism (Schikore, Schling, Oberbichler & Bauer 2020; Wan, Crolla & Schling 2025): constant joint spacing along the laths, laths perpendicular to the moving surface normal, rest bend kept with a chosen stiffness; driven by fixed / sliding supports, moved points or actuator cables, with a `Fold` slider and per-state drift / deviation / miss diagnostics. Every state is read elastically: bending and twist utilization of the lath section, strain energy Π = ½∫(GJτ² + EIκ²) ds, the natural (least-energy) state along the motion, and the strain envelope — each lath's peak over the whole motion, where it occurs and whether bending or twist governs (coloured per state in the web app). Exact on the doubly ruled hyperboloid grid (1e-12), settles traced nets in one step

### Utilities
- **Mesh Cleanup** — weld vertices (also far from the origin), reduce collapsed faces, drop degenerate/duplicate faces and isolated vertices, unify winding (closed parts outward, open parts by majority), collapse needles and flip caps below a `Sliver` angle, report non-manifold edges, Möbius-like parts and holes
- **Pull To Mesh** — project hand-drawn curves and points onto a mesh, with optional on-surface fairing
- **Mesh Colour Map** — colour a mesh by per-vertex values in one step (diverging palette centred on zero for signed curvature); the black / white / neon maps are shared with the web app and the bench (`Display/MitePalette` in the core) and are perceptually uniform: CIELAB lightness changes at a constant rate and both ends of the diverging map have the same lightness

All Grasshopper components weld coincident vertices on intake (STL imports, Brep meshes with seams) and map per-vertex results back to the original mesh, so **Mesh Colours** and **Deconstruct Mesh** line up.

Runtime messages say what was measured, why it matters and what to change, with the number to change it to: a Spacing below the mesh edge or above half the usable region, a Step that skips faces or neighbours, a MinAngle that leaves out the whole saddle (with the crossing angles found and a MinAngle that works), a mesh with no saddle at all, SeedPoints far off the mesh, Seeds out of range, and lath sections that look like a unit slip ("Width 80 × Thickness 12 … if you meant millimetres, enter Width 0.08 and Thickness 0.012").

## Quick Start

```csharp
using Mite.Core.Geometry;
using Mite.Core.Curvature;

// Load a mesh
var mesh = MeshData.LoadObj("model.obj");

// Compute principal curvatures
var result = PrincipalCurvature.Compute(mesh);
// result.K1, result.K2 — curvature values per vertex
// result.D1, result.D2 — curvature directions per vertex

// Compute Gaussian curvature
double[] K = GaussianCurvature.Compute(mesh);

// Compute mean curvature
var mean = MeanCurvature.Compute(mesh);
// mean.Values — scalar H per vertex
// mean.CurvatureNormals — mean curvature normal per vertex
```

## Grasshopper Components

Components appear under the **Mite** tab:

- **Curvature** — Principal Curvature, Gaussian Curvature, Mean Curvature, Curvature Streamlines, Umbilics, Test Shape
- **Form Finding** — Planarize Mesh, Minimal Surface, Force Density Method, Dynamic Relaxation
- **Gridshells** — Asymptotic Web, Asymptotic Net, Geodesic Net, Chebyshev Net, Conjugate Net, Geodesic Path, Mite Net (and the Mite Net parameter)
- **Analysis** — Lath Analysis, Gridshell Analysis, Mesh Isocurves
- **Fabrication** — Lath Sweep, Net Joints, Lath Unroll, Lath Segment, Lath Labels, Lath Preview, Flat Kit, Net Topology
- **Kinetics** — Net Kinetics
- **Util** — Mesh Cleanup, Pull To Mesh, Mesh Colour Map

**Modes are one right-click away.** Inputs that pick a mode rather than a
quantity (Layout, AutoSpace, Continuous, Upright, Shape, Palette, Symmetry, AAG …)
are listed in a submenu of the component's right-click menu with every choice
spelled out; picking one writes the input's own value (undoable), so saved
definitions and wires keep working. The active modes are written under the
component ("Fill · Continuous", "Upright · Rectangle"), and **Attach value lists**
wires a named drop-down into every unwired mode input.

A typical gridshell workflow: heal the mesh with **Mesh Cleanup**, trace a **Geodesic Net**
or **Asymptotic Net**, check strips with **Lath Analysis** and the whole network with
**Gridshell Analysis**, extrude with **Lath Sweep**, cut crossings with **Net Joints**,
split to stock with **Lath Segment**, and produce cutting patterns with **Lath Unroll**
plus IDs and a BOM from **Lath Labels**. With the `Net` wire the chain needs one connection per
component: the net output of the tracing component carries the surface and both families, and
a **Mite Net** in between adds the lath profile for all of them.

## Changelog

### Unreleased
- **Gridshell Analysis knows the stack** (`Layer` and `Align` inputs appended, `ReactionMoments` output appended; core `FrameAnalysis.Options.AxisOffsets`, `Result.ReactionMoments` / `Eccentricity` / `TotalMoment` / `MomentEquilibriumError`, `FrameAnalysis.OffsetStiffness`). Laths of a gridshell are stacked, so at a crossing the upper lath's axis runs one lath depth above the lower one and the bolt is a short rigid bar. With `Layer` (0 on the surface, 1 stacked on it …, one per curve) every lath keeps its own axis and every beam end hangs on a rigid link from its node on the surface — the rigid end offset of the frame programs (u_axis = u_node + θ × r, K_nodes = Hᵀ K H, loads carry their moment r × f; Oasys GSA theory, Midas, Karamba3D; the staggered-levels model of D'Amico, Kermani & Zhang 2014). A force handed between the layers at a crossing then acts with the lever arm of the stack: a horizontal pull of the upper lath twists the lower one by e·F, which the single node of 1.3.x could not see. Exact tests: on an L-frame the lower lath's torsion is e·q·b to 1e-10 and the reaction torque with it; offsets parallel to the load leave a determinate frame's forces unchanged to 1e-9; the rigid-link transfer is congruent and costs rigid motions nothing; a hyperboloid ruling grid in two layers balances forces and moments to 1e-12. On the bench's saddle net (80 × 12 mm upright, 1 kN/m wind) the stack raises the peak utilization 0.86 → 0.98 and lowers the displacement 2.12 → 2.04 mm. Without `Layer` nothing changes. The Report shows the eccentricity and a moment-equilibrium error.
- **Lath Analysis geodesic curvature and loop closure** (`Gridshells/LathAnalysis`, `Gridshells/FieldTracer.CloseSmoothly`; no input changes). `Kg` is now the in-surface turning of the lath summed over the window and divided by the arc between the end-chord mid-points, exact for a circle at any sampling; the two-chord angle it replaces was biased whenever the window (two mesh edges) was shorter than the curve's point spacing — a 40-point 45° latitude circle on a 48-division unit sphere read 1.36 instead of 1.00, a 30-point torus crown circle 0.58 instead of 0.33. Closed traced loops (Geodesic Net, Asymptotic Net, Conjugate Net, Curvature Streamlines) carried a facet-scale zigzag from the closure: the closure gap (the tracer's ~1 % drift) was spread as a constant 3D vector and reprojected, and half-way round a sphere that vector points into the surface, so each point landed on whichever facet beside the edge was nearer — ±1.2e-3 either side, in-plane kinks of 9°, `Kg` up to 1.12 on a great circle and a flat 100 × 10 mm lath at utilization 11 where the truth is 1.00. The gap is now spread in each point's tangent plane and the round-off stub before the closing point is dropped: kinks ≤ 0.4°, `Kg` ≤ 0.03, utilization 1.00; unfaired loops close without a kink. The strain model (fibre distances t/2 and w/2 by lath orientation, Saint-Venant twist, von Mises equivalence) was checked against Tang et al. 2016, Pottmann et al. 2010 and Schling et al. 2018 and kept; the sources are cited in the header of `LathAnalysis.cs`.
- **Nets seeded from a guide curve** (`EvenlySpacedNet.Options.Guide` / `GuideNodes`, `EvenlySpacedNet.GuideSeeds`, `WebSeeding.Guide` in the core; Grasshopper: an optional `Guide` curve input appended to Asymptotic Net, Conjugate Net, Curvature Streamlines and Geodesic Net). The curve is pulled onto the mesh — the pull slides to the foot on the smooth surface, because the facet-normal foot of a point well off the surface zigzags ±d·tan(dihedral/2) across a ridge and made a circle pulled from 0.5 outside a catenoid's waist 43 % too long — and nodes are placed every `Spacing` of arc length along it (centred on an open curve; L / round(L / Spacing) apart on a closed one). One curve of each family is traced through every node, border to border, so the net crosses at every node: Schling's layout (Schling, Hitrec & Barthel 2017 — the net drawn alternately from the nodes of the other family, the node-to-node distance along the curves being the one repeated parameter; Schling 2018 §4.3) and the origin points of the Studio X catalogue (along a middle curve, a diagonal pulled to the surface, a projected curve). Neighbouring curves are therefore `Spacing · sin(angle to the guide)` apart; a guide along one family's own curve seeds only the other family. Geodesics leave the guide with the first `Direction` carried along it by parallel transport, the condition for a family of geodesics emanating from a curve (Pottmann et al. 2010, *Geodesic patterns*, §2); round a closed guide the transport goes both ways so the holonomy of a non-geodesic loop lands opposite the seed. Verified: catenoid waist → 21 nodes 2π/21 apart (2e-5 rad), both asymptotic lines u ∓ v = const through every node (1e-5), 84 of 84 ends on the rims, offset from the closed form ≤ 0.0009 (edge 0.07); the same nodes from a circle drawn at r = 1.5 (9e-6); open meridian guide → nodes at the exact arc-length positions (8e-6); cylinder circle guide → 21 parallel 45° helices through the nodes, 2π/21 · sin 45° apart (±1e-4); sphere latitude guide → transported directions match the holonomy θ cos α to 0.011°; saddle diagonal x = y as the guide → family A untouched, family B one straight line x + y = const through each of 11 nodes exactly 0.25 apart. Web app: a `web · guide` layout using the horizontal section through the seed as the guide, its nodes drawn in the viewport.
- **Chebyshev Net: an exact compass step, and MinAngle against locking** (`Gridshells/ChebyshevNet`: `Options.MinAngle`, `Result.ShearAngles` / `EdgeError` / `Locked` / `Failed` added, the old constructor kept; Grasshopper: `MinAngle` input and `Report` output appended). Every interior node is now the surface point exactly one edge length from both parents — Newton's method in the tangent plane with reprojection onto the mesh, the compass rule of Otto, Hennicke & Matsushita (IL10, 1974) — and the axis geodesics are sampled by chord, so every edge of the net is L to round-off (η_len of Garg et al. 2014 ≈ 1e-14; before, eight fixed-point sweeps left edges 0.2–0.3 % off and nodes 1.4e-3 off their place at any mesh resolution). Checked against closed forms: the exact lattice on any flat triangulation (1e-15), the rolled lattice on a prism cylinder, and the closed-form compass net on the unit sphere (x·a = x·b = 1 − L²/2), which the mesh net matches to the depth of the facets below the sphere (5.1e-3 / 1.1e-3 / 3.0e-4 at 24 / 48 / 96 divisions — converging with the mesh, which it did not before). Hazzidakis (1880): a cell encloses ∫∫K dA = 2π − Σ(its four angles), so on a dome the shear angle falls ring by ring until the laths lock, and the compass then folds through zero instead of stopping; `MinAngle` (0 = off; Garg et al. use 30°) refuses every node that would create a crossing below it or above 180° minus it, so the net ends where it is still buildable (12 × 12 patch on the unit sphere at L = 0.2: 625 nodes shearing to 0.5° without it, 317 nodes with every crossing ≥ 32° at MinAngle 30°). `Report` gives the node count, the edge error, the shear range and the nodes refused; a remark names the locking when the net shears below 20°.
- **Flat Kit nests its strips** (`KitExport.Nest`, `Placement`, `Nesting`, `Split`, `LaneLength`, `StripArea`, `NestingSummary` in the core; `StripSheets`, `CutList`, `Files` and `Zip` keep their signatures and follow the nesting; Grasshopper: `Splice` input appended, the Report gains the nesting line; web app: the material use in the Kit summary and a "split long strips" switch). Strips go on the sheets first-fit decreasing by width then length, on the first shelf of the first sheet where they fit (Berkey & Wang 1987's finite first-fit; with one strip width each shelf is a one-dimensional bin, so FFD ≤ 11/9 OPT + 6/9, Dósa 2007) instead of the next-fit rule, which closed a row as soon as one strip did not fit (asymptotic ratio 1.691, Baker & Coffman 1981). The 3-fold Enneper kit at 1 : 5 takes 2 sheets of 600 × 400 mm instead of 3 — the area bound — at 61 % material use. A strip longer than the bed is laid along the bed's diagonal when it fits there (a 30 mm strip gets 661.7 mm on a 600 × 400 bed with a 4 mm gap; the lanes beside it take shorter strips end to end) and the diagonal is kept only when it costs no sheet; otherwise the strip is split into pieces (`A3/1`, `A3/2` …) lapped over `Splice` mm with two pin holes at the quarter points of each lap, the cut placed between two joints by `LathSegmentation.Segment` so the doubled thickness never meets a slot, and the lap shortened where the joints sit closer (three strip widths, else what the free stretch allows, 20 mm at least; laps that still reach a joint are counted and reported, never hidden). At 1 : 2.5 the same kit had 30 of 54 strips "longer than the bed — cut on a longer bed": now 7 sheets at 72 % use, 60 pieces lapped over 32 mm, no lap near a slot. Every sheet title states its material use; `Nesting.AreaBound` is the sheet count no nesting of those strips can beat. Labels on diagonal strips are rotated with them in SVG (`transform`) and DXF (group 50); the strip label now fits its free stretch.
- **Umbilics located, with their index** (`Umbilics.Locate` / `Degenerate` / `IndexSum` in the core, `Find` unchanged; Grasshopper: `Cancel` input appended, `Umbilics` and `Index` outputs appended; web app: the umbilics toggle also marks them, red +½ / blue −½, and the curvature line reports the index sum). The threshold on |k1 − k2| flags a region; the isolated umbilics are the faces round which the principal direction turns by ±180°: the index of the line field per face from the parallel-transported angle changes along the three edges plus the holonomy of the transport (Ray, Vallet, Li & Lévy 2008; Crane, Desbrun & Schröder 2010, N = 2), exactly a multiple of ½, summing to the Euler characteristic of a closed mesh; the point inside the face is the zero of the linearly interpolated deviator (Delmarcelle & Hesselink 1994), whose δ criterion agrees with the index. `Cancel` removes +½ / −½ pairs closer than a distance (facet noise in nearly umbilical regions; Tricoche, Scheuermann & Hagen 2001). Checked on the triaxial ellipsoid 1.5 × 1 × 0.7: exactly four umbilics, all lemons, index sum 2, each within 0.3–0.44 mesh edges of the closed-form position (±a √((a² − b²)/(a² − c²)), 0, ±c √((b² − c²)/(a² − c²))) on 48–144-segment meshes; torus none; the flat point of the monkey saddle a star at the origin; the poles of an ellipsoid of revolution index 2 in total.
- Test typology: the triaxial ellipsoid (`TestMeshes.CreateEllipsoid`; closed forms in `AnalyticShapes`: `EllipsoidUmbilics`, `EllipsoidCurvature` K = h⁴/(a²b²c²) and H, `EllipsoidPrincipal` exact shape operator, `EllipsoidConfocal` the Dupin coordinates). Mesh K and H within 1.4 % and principal directions within 0.7° of the closed form on 96 segments; Curvature Streamlines lie on the confocal quadric through their seed within 1 mesh edge (mean 0.1–0.2) for both families, also 1 edge from an umbilic, and the max-curvature line from (a, 0, 0) follows the umbilic connection y = 0 to 4e-4 edge and leaves it 0.44 edge past the umbilic.
- **Net Trim: cookie-cutting a net to a boundary** (new component, Mite > Gridshells; core `Gridshells/NetTrim`, additions only). Schling's gridshells are designed on a reference surface "larger than the actual structure" (AAG 2018 §2.2) and the web is "cropped" to the outline (CAD 2022 §4.2–4.3); Mite's tracers stop at the mesh border, so the mesh had to be the structure. Now: trace on the larger mesh, feed the families, the mesh and closed boundary curves (on the surface, or in plan with `Direction` 0,0,−1) to Net Trim, and get the pieces inside, the input index of each piece, the cut ends and the boundary as pulled onto the mesh. The boundary is pulled sample by sample (exact closest point or ray along `Direction`) until consecutive samples sit in edge-adjacent faces; those faces are a wall, the other vertices are flooded into regions, regions are joined across the wall and the odd-depth ones from the mesh border are the cookie — one loop keeps its inside, a loop inside a loop cuts a hole, a loop crossing the mesh border trims the corners and keeps the border ends, an open cut from border to border halves the surface. `Inside` points pick the regions instead, `Invert` keeps the outside. Every curve is cut at the exact crossing of its segment with the boundary (2D intersection in the local tangent plane, bisection on the side test as fallback); closed curves split without wrapping round their seam. Checked against closed forms: the straight asymptotic lines x ∓ y = c of z = x² − y² inside a circle (one piece each, ends x = c/2 ± ½√(2 − c²) and lengths √(2 − c²)√(2 + 4c²) within 5.5e-4 at edge 0.066 — the facet sagitta), inside an annulus (two pieces per line through the hole, ends within 1.1e-4 of a circle), the rulings of the hyperboloid between z = ±h (length 2h√2 within 4e-5), a non-convex loop (pieces equal to exact root finding), a traced classic net (every inside vertex kept, none outside), 0 of 3985 random points misclassified. Also handy for cutting an opening into an existing net (`Invert`) and for ending laths on a drawn support line.
- **Dynamic Relaxation follows Barnes** (`Dynamics/DynamicRelaxation`, new `Dynamics/LathBending`; Grasshopper: `Bending`, `RestLengths` inputs appended, `Laths`, `Curvature`, `Resets` outputs appended). The integrator is now Barnes' central-difference scheme (Barnes 1977 / 1999): fictitious nodal masses M = Δt² S / 2 from each node's direct stiffness (spring k + |T| / L, smoothing, soap film, bending), a half step from rest, and at every kinetic-energy peak the nodes are moved back to the peak position x − 3Δt/2 v + Δt²/2 R/M before the velocities are zeroed — before, every node had unit mass and only the velocities were zeroed. Same equilibria (within 3e-6 on a 20 × 20 hanging net), 3–6 × fewer steps (630 → 186 on that net, 1439 → 183 on an elastic chain, 1411 → 336 on a soap film between two rings, 34 043 → 5 860 on a buckled lath). ### 1.3.2
- **Geodesic Path: exact geodesics by edge flips** (`Gridshells/FlipGeodesic`, `Geometry/IntrinsicTriangulation` in the core; `Method` input appended, 1 = new default, 0 = the old curve shortening). The path is shortened on an intrinsic triangulation of the mesh with signposts (Sharp, Soliman & Crane 2019) by FlipOut (Sharp & Crane 2020) until every vertex it touches leaves ≥ 180° on both sides, then traced back across the faces as a polyline through every edge crossing: the geodesic of the polyhedral surface itself, with no step size or smoothing tolerance. It never passes a cone tip and passes a saddle vertex only where that is the geodesic (Mitchell, Mount & Papadimitriou 1987). The start path is the shortest path through 3 Steiner points per edge (Lanthier, Maheshwari & Sack 2001), so the way round each vertex is chosen on a graph within ~1 % of surface distances instead of the ~8 % of the edge graph; FlipOut is also run from the plain Dijkstra edge path and the shorter result kept. Checked against closed forms: on a polyhedral cone (exact development) 1000 of 1000 random pairs match to < 1e-13 and none touches the apex; prism cylinder, irregular flat triangulation and a single saddle vertex exact to 1e-12. 10–40× faster than the curve shortening on 2–20 k-vertex meshes; on a torus the old method went round the wrong side of the hole in some pairs (2 % too long), and its straight segments between surface points read lengths up to 0.2 % short on the cone. `Sampling` 0 now returns the exact polyline (a spacing > 0 gives the smooth interpolated curve as before); a non-manifold mesh falls back to Method 0 with a remark, inconsistent face winding is fixed first and bow-tie vertices are split.

### 1.3.0
- **Asymptotic Web** (new component, Mite > Gridshells; `Gridshells/AsymptoticWeb` in the core). An asymptotic parameterisation of a negatively curved surface is fixed up to u = f(ū), v = g(v̄) (Schling, Wang, Hoyer & Pottmann 2022), so placing nodes every Spacing along the two asymptotic curves through a seed fixes the whole net: every other node is the crossing of the curve of one family through a node of the first seed curve with the curve of the other family through a node of the second — Schling's "alternately drawing each curve and using their intersections as new starting points". No curve is inserted or stopped to keep a spacing, so there are no short stubs and no T-junctions; laths run border to border and the output includes the quad mesh of the nodes. The symmetry of the surface is carried into the net: a seed on a flat point (3-fold Enneper, monkey saddle) becomes a singular node where 2n asymptotic rays meet (found where the field points radially around the seed); a rotation about the seed normal is detected and one sector is traced and rotated; a surface of revolution gets N rotated copies of the two curves through the seed, N = round(2πρ / (√2·Spacing)), whose meridian diagonals are exact geodesics. Checked against the closed forms: catenoid nodes on the u ± v lattice to 0.6 % of Δφ, meridian diagonals planar to 1e-3; classical Enneper nodes on the u ± v lattice through the ray nodes to 3 % of the parameter step; the hyperbolic paraboloid web is its grid of rulings (straight to 0.01); 3-fold Enneper: six rays, 3-fold symmetry, crossings at 90° ± 3°.
- **AAG webs** (`Gridshells/AagWeb`, the AAG input of Asymptotic Web): the discrete model of Schling et al. 2022 — planar node stars n·(vᵢ − v) = 0 with |n| = 1, geodesic diagonals n·((v_a − v) × (v_c − v)) = 0, proximity to the reference surface through its tangent planes and fairness along the three families — solved by Levenberg–Marquardt on the sparse envelope solver. 3-fold Enneper: diagonal geodesic error 56.7° → 0.08°, star error 2.7° → 0.1°, the nodes move 0.5 % of the spacing on average (2 % at most). Rotational webs are AAG already (0.12° → 0.005°). Structurally the third family is decisive: the same 10 m Enneper web of 60 × 6 mm timber laths under self-weight and 0.5 kN/m² deflects 1.67 m as a quad web and 11 mm as an AAG web.
- **Gridshell Analysis** made exact at the joints: joints are inserted into every lath that passes through them at their exact position instead of snapping lath samples to them — the snap chained neighbouring joints together, so on a dense net most of the frame collapsed into a few nodes (a 3-fold Enneper net: 556 samples and 431 crossings became 62 nodes). New inputs (appended): SupportType (fixed / pinned), JointStiffness (rigid / scissor hinge / semi-rigid bolt about the surface normal), Density (self-weight), AreaLoad, LoadPoints and Forces; new outputs SupportNodes, Reactions and a Report with the load, the reactions and the equilibrium error. Torsion enters the utilisation (von Mises with τ = T·t/J); parts of the net no support reaches are left out and reported instead of making the frame singular. Closed-form tests: cantilever PL³/3EI to 1e-9, clamped beam qL⁴/384EI within 0.5 %, pinned cross (P/2)L³/48EI to 1e-9, a semi-rigid joint adds exactly PL²/k.
